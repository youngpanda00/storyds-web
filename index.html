The Lighthouse Keeper’s Daughter

Every night at nine, my father climbed the hundred and twelve steps to light the lamp. Every night at nine-oh-five, I climbed them too, counting out loud so he’d know I was coming.

He never turned around. He’d just say the next number before I could, like we were sharing one long sentence. Forty-one. Forty-two. By the top I’d be out of breath and he’d be polishing the brass, pretending he hadn’t been waiting.

The lamp room smelled of kerosene and salt. He taught me to trim the wick, to wipe the lens in circles, never lines, and to look at the water for a full minute before deciding the night was calm. “The sea lies in the first thirty seconds,” he said. “It tells the truth in the second thirty.”

The winter I turned twelve, a fishing boat came in out of a storm, steering by our light alone. The captain walked up the hill the next morning with a bucket of mackerel and shook my father’s hand for a long time without saying anything. That was when I understood that the lamp wasn’t a job. It was a promise made to strangers, renewed every night.

The year he got sick, I climbed the steps alone. I still counted out loud. Some nights I swore I heard him answer from the dark at the top, one number ahead of me, the way he always was.

The lighthouse is automatic now. A sensor turns on the lamp at dusk, and no one has to climb anything. I visit in the summers anyway. I take the stairs slowly, and I count.

Some habits are less about the stairs and more about who’s listening at the top.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Storyds free?

Yes. Reading and writing are free, and there are no ads.

Why the 1,200-word limit?

So every story fits in one sitting: a coffee, a commute, the minutes before sleep. It’s about a six-minute read, which is long enough for a twist and short enough to finish.

Do I need an account to read?

No. Open the app and start swiping. You’ll need an account to publish, love stories and keep them on your profile.

Who owns the stories I publish?

You do. Publishing gives Storyds permission to show your story in the app, and that ends when you delete the story or your account. See the Terms of Service.

Can I write dark stories?

Yes, there’s a whole Dark genre. Fiction can go to hard places; what isn’t allowed is content made only to shock or hurt. The Community Guidelines explain where the line is.

Is it on Android?

Not yet. Storyds is iPhone-only for now.
